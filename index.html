Where integrations break

A transfer crosses three systems before it is signed. Each one has a failure mode that returns no error.

  1. Origin wallet or dApp

    Fee asset resolution. An explicit choice is replaced by the account default on Hydration, and the quote silently switches asset.

  2. Exchange routing and quotes

    Client lifecycle. Cloned APIs lease pooled connections that are never released; an auto-selected swap can leak more than twenty leases per call.

  3. Destination signed XCM

    Calldata construction. On chains without DryRunApi, the final message can carry a placeholder fee amount of one whole token.

What a finding looks like

Across four public Polkadot wallets and transfer apps, the same checks found amounts truncated through a JS number, a destination-ED check comparing two different assets, and an agent swap tool that submitted only the first transaction of a two-leg route.

Fee asset precedence

High · reproduced

Two SDK implementations query MultiTransactionPayment.AccountCurrencyMap on Hydration and return the account's asset even when the caller passed a feeAsset. The third implementation guards against this; the other two were missed when the first was fixed.

Impact: the fee is quoted in, and returned as, an asset the caller did not choose. Three SDKs, two different answers for identical options. The fix is two lines; the regression test passes on one implementation and fails on the other two.

Client lifecycle

High · reproduced

Ten sdk-core call sites and six swap call sites clone an API and never disconnect. Only disconnect() decrements the pool's reference count, so single-use clients are treated as permanently in use.

Impact: connection churn, wasted keep-alive pings, and corrupted refcounts under sustained traffic. An auto-selected swap probes nine exchanges and leaks at least one client per candidate in a single call.

Signed placeholders

High · call-path proof

When the origin has no DryRunApi, fee computation returns null and the final type-and-then call is built as if it were a throwaway estimation pass: one whole fee token, half of it bought as execution, and an AllCounted filter.

Impact: on the five candidate chains whose origins report no DryRunApi support, the signed call carries a one-token fee amount and an AllCounted filter, so the account needs that headroom before the call is submitted. Whether the leftover fee asset is refunded or deposited is not established. The code comment calls it a dummy call that is "later dryRunned"; on these chains it is signed.

Questions

How is this different from the public bug bounty?

The bounty covers defects in the SDK itself. This audit covers how your integration uses it: your call paths, your assets, your failure handling. The report is yours, and nothing is disclosed publicly without your written agreement.

What do you need from me?

The repository and commit, the transfer paths and assets you care about, and whether you want the audit only or the audit with fixes. No credentials, no signing keys, no funds.

What if you find nothing?

The first verified finding is free. If the paid review finds nothing, the report still lists the paths checked, the checklist pass, and what could not be verified. Hypotheses are excluded from the finding count.

How long does it take?

Five business days after the scope is confirmed, with a 30-minute walkthrough. Audit plus fixes takes longer only when patches are included.

Is it confidential?

Yes. Findings are private to you, and public disclosure or a bounty submission is your decision. Analysis uses AI assistance and is reviewed by a human before delivery.

What is not promised?

No clean bill of health, no guarantee that the integration is bug-free afterwards, and no on-chain signing or custody. Every finding needs a failing test or a traced call path.
